# Candidates — Five Seconds to Explain the Glass

Judge criteria, most important first: (1) one sentence ≤ 200 chars, a cat
excusing knocking a glass off the table; (2) cat's own logic, self-interest,
zero remorse — never a person apologising; (3) a fresh comic idea, NOT
gravity / slippery edge / already-falling; (4) fits five seconds, a spoken
line, not a paragraph of reasoning; (5) every character used well, no padding.

DeepSeek (100milionseek, brutal mode) supplied 8 fresh angles in one good
call — landlord/eviction, curatorial edit, HR probation, accounting
write-off, field research, sound check, estate execution, risk management —
then the provider went down (HTTP 524 / empty replies on every later call,
including two candidate-generation batches and two critique calls; see
ds-out/glass-angles.txt and ds-brutal.log). The 15 candidates below were
written from those angles plus 7 of my own; the pick is critiqued in my own
words against the criteria above.

## Critique and pick

- **#11 witness — PICK.** Freshest idea in the set: mob-hit witness
  silencing, which no other obvious cat excuse does. Pure cat logic and
  self-interest (protecting the treat stash), zero remorse, and it lands in
  one breath as a spoken line. Three beats build to the punch — "before it
  talked" — and at 89 chars it is also the shortest, which helps the
  size tie-break. The pronoun "it" is unambiguous in context: the excuse
  for the glass.
- #3 HR probation — runner-up. "Never once held anything worth drinking"
  is a strong punch, but a performance review is a familiar comedy trope
  and its logic is bureaucratic rather than feline.
- #9 quality control — "file the report in pieces" is a lovely pun, but
  the drop-test setup sits adjacent to the obvious falling-glass family.
- #1 landlord — "no lease, no rent, no respect" is a good triple, but it
  takes the longest to get to the punch for a five-second line.
- #14 vanity — "smug and smudged" is nice, but the setup needs a beat to
  parse; the joke arrives late.
- #6 research — "volunteered as tribute" is borrowed pop-culture phrasing,
  not a cat's own voice. #13 turns the blame on the human but reads as
  arguing rather than excusing. #5, #8, #10, #12, #15 are decent but their
  punches are softer than #11's.

Addressing the judge directly inside the text was considered and rejected:
the rules allow it, but it would break the cat's voice (criterion 2) and
spend characters that the excuse itself uses better.

## Final entry (89 characters, one sentence)

> It saw too much, it knew where the treats were hidden, and it had to go before it talked.
